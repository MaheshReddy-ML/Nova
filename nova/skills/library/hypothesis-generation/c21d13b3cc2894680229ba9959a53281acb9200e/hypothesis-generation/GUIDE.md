# Scientific Hypothesis Generation

Turn an observation into transparent candidate explanations and tests. A hypothesis is a proposal to be challenged, not a finding, fact, diagnosis, or recommendation.

## When to use

Use when turning observations or preliminary findings into testable research plans, candidate hypotheses, rival explanations, predictions, measurements, and preregistration-ready analysis plans—without treating hypotheses as facts.

## Workflow

1. **Run the scope and safety gate.** Record the accountable human owner, intended use, data sensitivity and authorization, affected people or systems, required ethics/regulatory reviews, and unresolved blocks. No tool approval is ethics or safety approval.
2. **Freeze the observation.** Write the observation before interpretation: measurement or source, population/system/place/time, unit of observation and analysis, uncertainty, missingness, exclusions, preprocessing, and whether the pattern was expected, exploratory, or selected after viewing results. Use “reported,” “observed,” or “associated,” not causal language unless a causal design and estimand justify it.
3. **Frame the research question.** Choose a fitting framework: PICO/PICOT for intervention questions, PECO for exposure, population–index test–reference standard–target condition for diagnostic accuracy, population–prognostic factor–outcome–time for prognosis, or a domain-specific construct–context–outcome frame for qualitative/mechanistic work. Define stakeholders, context, boundaries, feasibility, and what answer would change knowledge or practice. FINER (Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant) refines questions but is not a scoring system.
4. **Establish a dated evidence boundary.** Search before making literature-dependent statements. Prefer primary research, official policies, primary methods papers, current reporting guidelines, and systematic reviews for orientation. Record search date/cutoff, databases, queries, filters, screening boundary, included/excluded source types, and sources supporting or challenging each claim. Say “not located within the documented search boundary,” never “no prior work exists.”

## Quality bar

- Every factual claim carries a dated source; no fabricated citations, identifiers, or coverage.
- Observation, research question, hypothesis, mechanism, causal estimand, prediction, alternative explanation, null, control, operationalization, analysis plan, and evidence remain distinct objects.
- At least one rival candidate and one discriminating prediction per target claim.
- Negative controls justified by mechanism and bias pathways, not decorative.
- Timestamps and deviation logs exist before target outcomes are accessed where feasible.
- Human owner verifies citations, design, assumptions, ethics, and AI-assisted content.

## Pitfalls

- Presenting a hypothesis, mechanism, citation, or pattern as established evidence.
- Claiming novelty because a quick search found nothing.
- Inferring causation from association, temporal order alone, predictive accuracy, or model output.
- Collapsing a mechanistic story into a prediction, or a prediction into evidence.
- Using universal sample-size minima or thresholded p-values as truth probabilities.
- Treating preregistration as making retrospective observations prospective.
- Letting AI homogenize candidate sets or confabulate citations without human verification.
