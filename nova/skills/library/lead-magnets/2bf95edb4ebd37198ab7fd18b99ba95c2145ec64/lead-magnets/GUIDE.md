# Lead Magnets

One sentence: NOVA can design high-converting lead magnets that capture qualified emails by matching format, problem, and buyer stage to your product and audience.

## When to use

Use this skill when planning a new lead magnet, improving an existing offer, deciding what to give away for emails, or determining appropriate gating and form fields. Also use it to audit a current lead magnet’s alignment with buyer stage, perceived value, and natural product path.

## Workflow

1. **Gather product and audience context** — Ask: What does the company do? Who is the ideal customer? What problems does the product solve? What is the current conversion rate on email capture? What existing content could be repurposed?

2. **Identify the target buyer stage** — Determine if the goal is awareness (education), consideration (evaluation), or decision (implementation). This dictates format and depth.

3. **Select the lead magnet type** — Choose from checklist, cheat sheet, template, swipe file, ebook/guide, mini-course, quiz, webinar, or resource library based on the problem, effort, and time-to-create trade-offs. Match the format to the buyer stage using the provided tables.

4. **Define the gating strategy** — Decide between full gate, partial gate, ungated + optional, or content upgrade. Specify form fields (email only, email + name, email + company/role) and frame the exchange with clear value, preview, social proof, and low-risk language.

5. **Design the landing page and delivery** — Structure the page with headline, preview, what’s inside, social proof, minimal form, and FAQ. Choose delivery method (instant download, email delivery, thank you page + email, or drip) based on the desired relationship start.

6. **Plan promotion and distribution** — Add content upgrades within relevant blog posts, use exit-intent popups, share teasers on social media, and consider paid amplification if budget allows. Align channels with audience habits.

7. **Establish measurement metrics** — Track landing page conversion rate, cost per lead, lead-to-customer rate, email engagement, and time to conversion. A/B test headline, format, gate level, form fields, CTA copy, and delivery method. Evaluate lead quality by email engagement, progression to trial/demo, unsubscribe rate, and ICP match.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Lead magnet solves one specific, painful problem, not a broad topic
- [ ] Format matches the buyer stage (awareness, consideration, decision)
- [ ] Perceived value is high while time investment is low (consumable in under 30 minutes)
- [ ] Natural path to product is clear: problem solved by lead magnet is also solved by the product
- [ ] Easy to consume: single format, mobile-friendly, no special software required
- [ ] Gating asks for minimum fields; every extra field reduces conversion by 5-10%
- [ ] Landing page includes headline, preview, key takeaways, social proof, form, and FAQ
- [ ] Distribution plan includes at least two promotion channels relevant to the audience
- [ ] Measurement plan defines primary KPI (conversion rate, cost per lead, or lead-to-customer rate) and one A/B test priority

## Pitfalls

- **Too broad a topic**: "Marketing guide" instead of "How to write cold emails that get replies"
- **Mismatched stage**: Offering a deep ebook to awareness-stage visitors who need education, not evaluation
- **High friction gating**: Asking for company name, job title, phone number on a simple checklist; every extra field drops conversion 5-10%
- **Low perceived value**: Delivering a thin, poorly designed PDF that looks like it’s not worth the exchange
- **No clear product path**: Lead magnet solves a problem the product does not, or the connection is vague
- **Single-channel promotion**: Relying only on a sidebar CTA without content upgrades, social teasers, or paid amplification
- **Missing measurement**: Launching without defining conversion rate targets, A/B test priorities, or lead quality signals
