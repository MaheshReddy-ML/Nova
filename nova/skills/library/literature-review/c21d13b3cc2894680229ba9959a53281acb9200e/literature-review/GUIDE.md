# literature-review

One sentence: Conducts systematic, scoping, and narrative literature reviews using academic databases, tracks search coverage and evidence limitations, and generates professional markdown and PDF output with verified citations.

## When to use

- Conducting a systematic literature review for research or publication
- Synthesizing current knowledge on a specific topic across multiple sources
- Performing meta-analysis or scoping reviews
- Writing the literature review section of a research paper or thesis
- Investigating the state of the art in a research domain
- Identifying research gaps and future directions
- Requiring verified citations and professional formatting

## Workflow

1. **Define question and criteria**: Establish the research question, inclusion/exclusion criteria, and scope before searching.
2. **Systematic literature search**: Conduct multi-database searching (PubMed, arXiv, bioRxiv, Semantic Scholar) with recorded queries and result counts.
3. **Screening and selection**: Perform title/abstract screening followed by full-text screening, maintaining PRISMA flow counts for records, reports, and studies.
4. **Data extraction and quality assessment**: Extract structured data and conduct risk-of-bias or quality appraisal using appropriate tools.
5. **Synthesis and analysis**: Synthesize findings thematically or quantitatively across studies, comparing, contrasting, and identifying patterns.
6. **Citation verification**: Verify every citation against the actual source using DOI checks and metadata retrieval.
7. **Document generation**: Assemble the review with a complete bibliography in markdown, then generate a professional PDF.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Search documented with strings, dates, and result counts for reproducibility
- [ ] Multiple databases searched to avoid coverage gaps
- [ ] Preprint servers included (bioRxiv, medRxiv, arXiv)
- [ ] Deduplication performed on records, then linked by study
- [ ] PRISMA flow tracked: records screened, reports sought/not retrieved/assessed, reports excluded with reasons, included studies
- [ ] Inclusion/exclusion criteria documented and applied consistently
- [ ] Quality assessment performed using appropriate tools
- [ ] Every citation verified against actual source (DOI, metadata, claim support)
- [ ] Synthesis organized thematically, not study-by-study
- [ ] Limitations, gaps, and bias acknowledged
- [ ] Professional formatting with complete bibliography

## Pitfalls

1. **Single database search**: Misses relevant papers; always search multiple databases
2. **No search documentation**: Makes review irreproducible; document all searches
3. **Study-by-study summary**: Lacks synthesis; organize thematically instead
4. **Treating DOI existence as support**: A registered DOI can still identify the wrong work; check identity, claim support, corrections, and retractions
5. **Too broad search**: Yields thousands of irrelevant results; refine with specific terms
6. **Too narrow search**: Misses relevant papers; include synonyms and related terms
7. **Ignoring preprints**: Misses latest findings; include bioRxiv, medRxiv, arXiv
8. **No quality assessment**: Treats all evidence equally; assess and report quality
9. **Publication bias**: Only positive results published; note potential bias
10. **Outdated search**: Field evolves rapidly; clearly state search date
