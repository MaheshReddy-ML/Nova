# marketing-psychology

One sentence: NOVA applies psychological principles and mental models to explain why customers buy, how to influence behavior ethically, and how to make better marketing decisions using proven frameworks and criteria.

## When to use

Use this skill when the user asks why buyers behave as they do, whether a persuasion tactic is honest, or wants to apply psychology to marketing, pricing, copy, or page optimization. Reference `.agents/product-marketing.md` first if it exists to tailor recommendations to the specific product and audience.

## Workflow (numbered steps adapted to NOVA's tools)

1. **Identify the user's goal**: Determine if they need buyer psychology insights, persuasion tactics, pricing psychology, or design models. Check product-marketing context for audience specifics.

2. **Select relevant mental models**: Choose 2-3 models from the foundations, buyer psychology, or influencing sections that directly address the situation. Prioritize models with clear marketing applications and ethical guidelines.

3. **Explain the psychology**: For each selected model, briefly describe the core principle using established frameworks (e.g., "Loss aversion: losses feel ~2x as painful as equivalent gains per prospect theory").

4. **Provide specific applications**: Give 1-2 concrete marketing uses per model, grounded in numbers or heuristics where available (e.g., "charm pricing: prices ending in 9 seem significantly lower than round numbers per the left-digit effect").

5. **Add ethical guardrails**: Note when a tactic should only be used when genuine (e.g., scarcity requires real limited availability) and suggest testing with real customers per the skill's question framework.

## Quality bar (checklist)

- [ ] References specific mental models with correct terminology and origins
- [ ] Provides marketing applications grounded in heuristics, numbers, or established criteria
- [ ] Includes ethical guardrails or conditions for honest application
- [ ] Avoids suggesting scripts, files, packages, other tools, sub-agents or IDEs
- [ ] Requires sources for any factual claim about consumer behavior or study results
- [ ] Output is concise (1-3 sentences per model application)

## Pitfalls

- **Overapplying models**: Forcing a mental model onto a situation where it doesn't fit, leading to irrelevant or manipulative tactics
- **Ignoring ethical boundaries**: Using scarcity, urgency, or loss aversion when the constraint isn't genuine, which damages trust
- **Confusing models**: Attributing results to the wrong psychological principle (e.g., calling social proof "authority bias")
- **Skipping customer testing**: Assuming a model works without validating with the actual audience, which the skill's task-specific questions explicitly warn against
- **Inventing facts**: Claiming study results or statistics without citing sources; the skill requires evidence for factual claims about buyer behavior
