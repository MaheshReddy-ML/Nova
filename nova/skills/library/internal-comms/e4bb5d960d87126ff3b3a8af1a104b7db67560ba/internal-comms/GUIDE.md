# internal-comms

One sentence: NOVA writes structured internal communications—status reports, leadership updates, 3P updates, newsletters, FAQs, and incident reports—using company-approved formats and tone.

## When to use

- Writing 3P updates (Progress, Plans, Problems) for teams.
- Drafting company newsletters or all-hands communications.
- Creating FAQ responses for common employee questions.
- Composing status reports for project stakeholders.
- Producing leadership updates or executive summaries.
- Documenting incident reports or project post-mortems.

## Workflow

1. **Identify communication type** – Determine if the request matches 3P updates, a company newsletter, an FAQ, a status report, a leadership update, a project update, or an incident report.
2. **Select guideline file** – Load the corresponding example from the `examples/` directory: `3p-updates.md` for Progress/Plans/Problems team updates, `company-newsletter.md` for company-wide newsletters, `faq-answers.md` for FAQ responses, or `general-comms.md` for all other formats.
3. **Follow formatting instructions** – Apply the specific instructions from the selected guideline for structure, tone, and content gathering.
4. **Gather required content** – Use NOVA's `web_search` or `deep_research` to find up-to-date data, metrics, or company announcements needed for the draft.
5. **Draft using template** – Write the communication following the template's sections, ensuring the tone matches company style (typically professional, concise, and forward-looking).
6. **Review against quality bar** – Check that all required sections are present, data is accurate and sourced, and the format adheres to the guideline.
7. **Present for owner confirmation** – Output the draft and ask the owner to review before any distribution.

## Quality bar

- [ ] Communication type is correctly identified and matches the selected guideline.
- [ ] All required template sections are included (e.g., Progress, Plans, Problems for 3P updates).
- [ ] Key data, metrics, or dates are accurate and sourced from reliable references.
- [ ] Tone is consistent with company style: professional, concise, and appropriate for the audience.
- [ ] Format follows the guideline's structure (headings, bullet points, tables as specified).
- [ ] No unsupported claims; every factual statement has a verifiable source.
- [ ] Draft is ready for owner review and approval before sending.

## Pitfalls

- **Mismatched format** – Using the wrong guideline file leads to missing sections or incorrect structure.
- **Missing sources** – Including metrics or claims without citations violates the accuracy requirement.
- **Wrong tone** – Overly casual language in leadership updates or overly formal tone in quick team status reports can reduce clarity.
- **Skipping the owner review step** – Distributing drafts without confirmation can spread misaligned messaging.
- **Ignoring audience needs** – Failing to tailor complexity and detail level for the intended recipients (e.g., engineering team vs. executive leadership).
