# Content Strategy
This skill makes NOVA better at planning content that drives traffic, authority, and leads by mapping topic clusters, buyer stages, and editorial priorities to customer research and search demand.

## When to use
Use when the user wants a content plan, topic prioritization, an editorial roadmap, or an explanation of why posts are not converting. Do not use for writing individual pieces (copywriting), technical SEO audits (seo-audit), or social execution (social).

## Workflow (numbered steps adapted to NOVA's tools)
1. Read product-marketing context if available using read_website or local files; gather business context, customer research, current state, and competitive landscape by asking the owner or using web_search/deep_research.
2. Identify 3–5 content pillars: product-led problems, audience needs, search volume, and competitor rankings. Confirm each pillar aligns with the product, matches audience interest, has search or social interest, and is broad enough for subtopics.
3. Map keyword topics to buyer stages using modifiers: awareness ("what is," "how to"), consideration ("best," "vs," "alternatives"), decision ("pricing," "reviews," "demo"), and implementation ("templates," "tutorial," "setup").
4. Source ideas from keyword exports, call transcripts, surveys, forums (site:reddit.com, site:quora.com), competitor blogs (site:competitor.com/blog), and sales/support input. Extract questions, pain points, objections, and voice-of-customer language.
5. Score each idea: Customer Impact 40%, Content-Market Fit 30%, Search Potential 20%, Resource Requirements 10%. Score 1–10 per factor, multiply by weight, sum, and rank.
6. Apply the information-gain gate for searchable pieces: use web_search or deep_research to read current top results, then write one sentence on what your piece adds that none of them have (original data, product example, tool, clearer answer, stronger opinion). If you cannot, do not write the piece.
7. Build the calendar split: 60% searchable, 30% shareable, 10% experimental. Adjust for new blogs (more searchable) or established brands chasing category leadership (more shareable).
8. Plan per-format discipline: 10 title options before drafting blog posts, ~5 editing passes, hub before spokes for hub-and-spoke clusters, and distribution hooks built into creation.

## Quality bar (checklist)
- Product-marketing context read first if available
- 3–5 pillars, each tied to product, audience, search/social interest, and breadth
- Every searchable target passes the information-gain gate with a sourced differentiator
- Topics mapped to awareness, consideration, decision, or implementation stage
- Ideas scored with the 40/30/20/10 weighted model and ranked
- Calendar follows 60/30/10 split or a justified deviation
- Hub built before spokes; strategic interlinking planned
- Distribution hooks planned at creation, not after
- Factual claims require sources; no invented metrics
- Output saved as long-form Markdown via save_document

## Pitfalls
- Treating content as overhead instead of brand surface area; each piece is an entry point and must be distributed.
- Writing the eleventh copy of a search result without information gain; neither Google nor AI assistants have a reason to prefer it.
- Spray-and-pray distribution across platforms with no flagship and no repurposing plan.
- Over-investing in rented channels; organic reach on social platforms can collapse overnight, while owned email and blog assets convert durably.
- Expecting links from formats that do not earn them; statistics pages earn ~4.25x backlinks relative to page share, while ultimate guides and thought leadership earn less.
- Building dedicated hub URLs for every topic; most content works under /blog with internal linking.
- Skipping customer research and optimizing for keywords that no buyer actually asks about.
