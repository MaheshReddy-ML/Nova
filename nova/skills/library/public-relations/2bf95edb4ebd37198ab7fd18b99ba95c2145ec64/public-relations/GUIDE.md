# Public Relations & Earned Media

One sentence: NOVA learns to identify newsworthy angles, craft journalist-grade pitches, and build media lists that earn coverage without spamming reporters.

## When to use

- When you have a real story (proprietary data, strong opinion, milestone, customer before/after, or fresh angle on a trend).
- When founder/exec time is available for quotes (journalists reject form-rep responses).
- When you have a destination (press page or blog) that converts attention.
- For reactive newsjacking (hours to days), proactive pitching (2–8 weeks), inbound HARO queries, or maintaining an owned press page.

## Workflow (numbered steps adapted to NOVA's tools)

1. **Check product-marketing context** — if `.agents/product-marketing.md` exists, read it first to align messaging.
2. **Validate newsworthiness** against the Quick Reference Pitch Quality Bar: journalist beat fit, clear news hook, complete story possible from pitch alone, specific subject line, <150 words, no banned words ("revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive," "synergy"), clear ask.
3. **Select a story angle** — Founding Story, David vs Goliath, or Have an Enemy (a broken system, never a competitor). Reference `references/story-angles.md` for taxonomy.
4. **Build a scored media list** — use `web_search` to find journalists covering the beat in the last 5 articles; score each by relevance, recent activity, and outlet authority.
5. **Draft the pitch** — subject line specific enough to predict the headline; body under 150 words; lead with the news hook, not the product; include data, a quoteable customer name, and contact info; avoid hype words.
6. **Send via `draft_email`** — NOVA drafts; the owner confirms before sending. Always include a press@ email or clear reply path.
7. **Follow up once** after 48 hours if no response; then move on. Track coverage, domain referral traffic, and brand search lift.
8. **Update memory** — save successful pitches, journalist responses, and coverage URLs via `save_memory` for future angle scoring.

## Quality bar (checklist)

- [ ] Does this journalist cover this beat? (Check their last 5 articles.)
- [ ] Is there a clear news hook — something that just happened or is about to?
- [ ] Could this journalist write a complete story from this email alone? (Data, quotes, customer name, contact.)
- [ ] Is the subject line specific enough to predict the article's headline?
- [ ] Is the pitch under 150 words?
- [ ] Did you avoid the words "revolutionary," "game-changing," "disruptive," and "synergy"?
- [ ] Is the ask clear? (Interview? Embargo? Exclusive? Quote?)

Pitfalls

- Pitching without beat fit: journalists ignore irrelevant pitches; always verify recent coverage.
- Hype-heavy language: banned words trigger immediate deletion; lead with data or conflict instead.
- Skipping the follow-up: one follow-up after 48 hours is standard; more risks spamming.
- Treating PR as a traffic play: earned media compounds authority and backlinks, not direct conversions; manage expectations.
- Ignoring the press page: a missing or broken `/press` page signals amateurism; set it up once and keep it current.
