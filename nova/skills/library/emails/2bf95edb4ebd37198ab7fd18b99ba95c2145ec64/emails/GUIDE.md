# emails

One sentence: NOVA can design, audit, and refine email sequences—welcome, nurture, re-engagement, and onboarding—using proven frameworks for timing, structure, and conversion rather than generic AI outputs.

## When to use

- User asks what to send in a welcome, nurture, or win-back flow and when.
- User wants to audit an existing sequence for missing emails, weak CTAs, or poor timing.
- User needs to write or edit individual emails with better structure, tone, or personalization.
- User requests subject lines, preview text, or body copy that follows copy guidelines and avoids AI tells.

## Workflow (numbered steps adapted to NOVA's tools)

1. **Identify sequence type** – Ask the user for the flow kind (welcome, nurture, re-engagement, onboarding) and confirm the trigger (signup, inactivity, post-purchase, etc.).
2. **Load reference** – If available, read `.agents/product-marketing.md` or `.claude/product-marketing.md` to extract learnings, angles, and previously tested offers so NOVA does not re-propose them.
3. **Audit or outline** – Use the sequence-type overview (welcome: 5–7 emails over 12–14 days; nurture: 6–8 over 2–3 weeks; re-engagement: 3–4 over 2 weeks; onboarding: 5–7 over 14 days) to structure the number of emails and ideal delays.
4. **Draft email copy** – For each email, apply the Core Principles: one job per email, value before ask, clear path forward. Write a subject line (clear > clever, 40–60 chars), preview text (~90–140 chars), and body (hook, context, value, CTA, sign-off). Follow the Structure, Formatting, and Tone guidelines; avoid AI tells, stock phrases, and em-dash overuse.
5. **Review against quality bar** – Checklist: single primary CTA, benefit-driven subject, no negation lists, mobile-first formatting, short paragraphs, human tone, no stock openers or sign-off filler.
6. **Present output** – Give the user a Sequence Overview and each Email’s details (send timing, subject, preview, body, CTA, segment/conditions). Include a Metrics Plan with primary conversion goal and suggested benchmarks.

## Quality bar (checklist)

- [ ] One primary purpose and one main CTA per email.
- [ ] Subject line is clear, specific, 40–60 characters, benefit or curiosity-driven, no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- [ ] Preview text extends the subject, is 90–140 characters, and does not repeat the subject.
- [ ] Body follows: Hook → Context → Value → CTA → Sign-off; short paragraphs (1–3 sentences); white space; bullet points for scanability; mobile-first.
- [ ] Tone is conversational, first/second person, active voice; reads like a real person.
- [ ] No AI tells: no "It's not X, it's Y" contrasts, no "No X, no Y, no Z" lists, no trailing pile-ons, no self-answered colon reveals, no stock phrases ("Say goodbye to," "Unlock the power of," etc.).
- [ ] No more than one or two em-dashes in the body; none in subject or preview.
- [ ] Value is delivered before any ask; email moves the reader to a useful next step.
- [ ] Segmentation or conditions noted if the email is targeted.

## Pitfalls

- Assuming a fixed email count without considering sales cycle length, product complexity, or relationship stage.
- Using vague or clever subject lines instead of clear, benefit-driven ones; defaulting to fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" prefixes.
- Repeating the subject line in preview text or writing stock openers ("I hope this email finds you well," "Just checking in").
- Overloading one email with multiple jobs or asks; more than one primary CTA per email.
- Ignoring timing norms (welcome immediate, nurture 2–4 days apart, re-engagement 30–60 day trigger) or time zone considerations.
- Producing copy that sounds AI-generated: long paragraphs, formal tone, stock phrases, em-dash clutter, or negation lists.
- Skipping the product-marketing context check, risking re-proposal of already-tested angles or offers.
