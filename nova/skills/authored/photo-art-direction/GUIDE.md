# Photo art direction

Real photos are the strongest trust signal on a small-business site; choose and describe them deliberately.

## Choosing
- The business's own photos always come first, even when stock looks more polished. Usable: sharp, at least 1000 px,
  not a logo, flyer, menu scan, screenshot or text-heavy social graphic.
- Hero = the experience the customer buys (hands on clay, the plated dish, the stylist at work), one clear subject,
  natural light, calm space for text. Never a social card, a logo or the building exterior unless that is the draw.
- Layout follows inventory: 0 photos typographic; 1-3 hero + story with text cards; 4-7 photo-led offering cards;
  8+ add a gallery.

## Sequencing
1. Hero: the core experience.
2. Offerings: one photo per offering showing exactly that thing.
3. Story: the people or the place.
4. Gallery: alternate detail and wide shots; keep colour temperature consistent; never repeat a photo.

## Photo subjects for stock search (only when they have fewer than 4 usable photos)
- Give 3-6 concrete subjects of 2-5 words: hero first, then one per offering in order.
- Name the visible activity and material: "hands shaping clay wheel", "resin pour coasters", "cake piping
  decorating", "acoustic open mic stage". Not "art", "fun", "business", "happy people".
- Avoid clichés: handshakes, laptops, staged laughter, thumbs up, skylines, watermarks, 3D renders.
- Stock must allow commercial use and modification, be credited, and be flagged to the client as placeholders.

## Alt text
- 5-15 words describing what is visible and why it matters: "Hands shaping a bowl on a pottery wheel".
- Never "image", "photo 1", file names or keyword stuffing; decorative duplicates get empty alt.
